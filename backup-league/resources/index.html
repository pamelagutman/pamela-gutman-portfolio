Design for engagement & impact.

Escape-room-style learning works best when solving the challenge also means practicing a meaningful action.

Companion resources for Design for Engagement & Impact: Escape-Room-Style Learning Tied to Real Performance Tasks, presented by Pamela Gutman.

Start with performance, not a padlock

A five-step design approach

01

Choose a real decision

What do learners need to notice, decide, or do differently back on the job?

02

Make the choice observable

What would successful performance look like? What would a plausible mistake look like?

03

Build a meaningful challenge

Give players enough information, realistic constraints, and an interaction that reveals their reasoning.

04

Design feedback and hints

Let teams check their own progress. Preserve the fun of discovery without making directions a mystery.

05

Playtest, revise, debrief

Watch for confusion, not just wrong answers. Debrief the decisions, not merely the puzzle solutions.

A principle from our own playtesting: Make the puzzle challenging, but make the interaction intuitive. If players cannot tell which materials to use or what to do next, that is friction, not learning.

The Backup League case study

One storyline. Three different kinds of thinking.

01

Prioritize information

A dispatch incident board asks players to find the emergencies and put them in deadline order.

Mechanic: choose, arrange, flip, verify.

02

Decide under constraints

A rotating wheel lets players test configurations against a set of operational requirements.

Mechanic: manipulate, compare, commit.

03

Identify risk

Visual word riddles and UV markings reveal evidence that challenges an apparently safe decision.

Mechanic: discover, evaluate, choose.
