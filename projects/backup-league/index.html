The Backup League

What happens when the superheroes disappear and the people who keep headquarters running have to save the day?

A tabletop micro escape challenge designed to turn three workplace performance behaviors into hands-on decisions.

The design challenge

For a 45-minute conference session, I wanted participants to experience an escape-room-style activity that did more than entertain. The puzzles needed to require meaningful decisions, work for small groups in a conventional conference room, and run without constant facilitator intervention.

The fictional superhero headquarters makes the experience playful while allowing the underlying skills to transfer to workplaces, higher education, and other settings.

Constraints that shaped the design

  • About five players per team
  • Short, self-contained tabletop activity
  • Chairs-only setup must be possible
  • Self-checking solutions and optional hints
  • Printable, reproducible materials
  • Phone-accessible final override

Mechanics with a purpose

Three missions, three performance behaviors

01 · Prioritize information

Memory Meltdown

Identify the three incidents needing immediate attention, weigh their deadlines and dependencies, then assemble a physical dispatch-console reveal.

02 · Decide under constraints

Gadget Graveyard

Use four specialist reports to calibrate a rotating generator wheel. One setting satisfies the power, heat, and AI-isolation requirements.

03 · Identify risk

Academy Confidential

Solve word-picture riddles, reveal invisible-ink evidence, and challenge a misleading safety clearance before making a decision.

What playtesting changed

Early testing showed that players enjoyed the tactile mechanics, but unfamiliar terminology, overly elaborate classification rules, and unclear transitions created unnecessary cognitive load.

I reduced the dispatch task to three meaningful selections, made dependencies explicit, simplified the Academy paperwork, and retained the satisfying physical reveals. The goal was to keep the thinking challenging while making the instructions intuitive.

How the experience ends

Each mission yields a star-chart fragment. Players combine the three fragments to uncover a five-symbol rescue sequence and enter it into a phone-friendly H.E.L.P.E.R. override console.

This is a prototype and design case study. The final learning outcomes and timing will be assessed through further playtesting.
