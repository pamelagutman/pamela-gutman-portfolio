A superhero finale with four possible endings

A hybrid scavenger hunt brought remote and on-site staff into the same adventure, then let teams choose how the final showdown played out.

An interactive Storyline finale created for Customer Service Week, with superhero artwork and a final decision that actually changed the ending.

Project context

Customer Service Week: hybrid scavenger hunt

A staff connection activity that ended with a choose-your-outcome superhero showdown.

What
Daily themed team challenges and a branching finale
When
Fall 2022 · Customer Service Week
Where
Hybrid: participants joining both remotely and on-site
Tools
Articulate Storyline, Adobe Illustrator, third-party video

The challenge

Many colleagues had joined remotely during the pandemic. Some reported feeling isolated or unsure how to navigate campus resources and relationships.

Our Customer Service Week scavenger hunt gave people a shared reason to explore, work together, and discover both people and places, while making room for remote colleagues to participate meaningfully.

How the finale worked

  • Daily challenges built toward a superhero-themed confrontation.
  • Teams caught up with the villain at the final showdown.
  • Their choices changed the resolution, leading to four possible endings.
  • The finale was built in Storyline; graphics were created or adapted in Illustrator.

Why the interaction mattered

Don't just tell the team they won. Let their choices count.

The branching finale turned a themed celebration into an interactive decision. Rather than watching a predetermined ending, teams could see how their choices shaped the conclusion.

I developed the game in Articulate Storyline, using graphics created or adapted in Illustrator, with a guest video from YouTube creator Mightyraccoon.

Shared stakes. Remote and on-site participants worked toward the same goal.

Meaningful choices. Four possible endings gave teams a visible payoff.

Resource discovery. The challenges encouraged participants to locate information and contacts they could use afterward.

What I heard afterward

More connection, fewer "where do I find...?" questions.

In my original portfolio notes, I reported staff saying they felt more engaged and connected, fewer requests about common resources, and interest in future themes.

These are qualitative impressions and operational observations rather than controlled outcome measures.

The transferable principle

A good finale pays off the decisions that came before it.

Branching doesn't have to be enormous to feel meaningful. A few well-designed choices can turn an activity into a story participants helped write.
