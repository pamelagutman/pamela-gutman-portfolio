The learner experience of virtual onboarding

What was it actually like to start a new job online during the pandemic, and what could those experiences teach us about onboarding afterward?

A semester-long team research project, presented to a doctoral-program audience, that strengthened my approach to designing for real learners instead of assuming we know what they need.

Research questions

Listen before redesigning.

Our team surveyed people internationally about beginning new positions online. We wanted to describe the experience of workplace onboarding, explore preferences and recurring themes, and identify lessons that could inform onboarding beyond the pandemic.

What did newcomers experience? Understanding the practical and social realities of virtual onboarding.

What patterns emerged? Looking for themes in preferences and experiences rather than relying on assumptions.

What could we change? Turning the evidence into questions and design ideas for future onboarding programs.

Methods & process

From an approved study to a shared story

01

Research ethics

The team obtained institutional review board (IRB) approval for research involving human participants.

02

Recruitment

We used snowball sampling, inviting people through social networks and encouraging them to share the survey.

03

Analysis

We collected survey responses in Qualtrics and looked for patterns and themes in the onboarding experiences reported.

04

Communication

We designed a Google Slides presentation and included Mentimeter and Padlet activities in the online delivery.

Snowball recruitment is a non-probability sampling method. This project is a useful source of learner perspectives, not a representative estimate of all new employees.

What I contributed

Every team member participated across the research process. My particular focus was analyzing the survey data to identify themes and designing the presentation so our audience could engage with the results rather than simply sit through them.

That combination of research, analysis, and communication matters to me: insights are more useful when the people making decisions can understand and act on them.

What happened afterward

In my original project write-up, I recorded positive feedback on the presentation's relevance and engagement from students and instructors. I also described applying the research to the hybrid structure of an organizational onboarding experience.

Those are reflections reported in the original portfolio, not published statistical findings from the survey.
