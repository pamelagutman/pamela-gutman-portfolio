What the talk covered

The session explores a four-step process: brainstorm, narrow the focus, iterate and refine, and finalize and prototype. The goal is to speed up ideation without surrendering design judgment.

It also includes examples of ways AI can help with themes, puzzle design, branching scenarios, and “what if” testing for learning experiences.

Why it matters

  • It helps beat blank-page syndrome.
  • It makes rapid prototyping easier.
  • It creates room for more iteration.
  • It still leaves the human in charge.

Behind the finished work

My role, decisions & evidence

A little more about what I contributed and how I evaluated the design.

My role
Developed a session demonstrating how generative AI can support ideation, iteration, and prototyping of gamified learning experiences.
Design decision
Treat AI as a creative collaborator that expands the option space, while keeping learning objectives, accuracy checks, and final judgments with the human designer.
Evidence boundary
This page explains a workflow and demonstrations, not a claim that AI automatically improves learning outcomes or replaces evaluation and playtesting.

The transferable principle

What another designer can borrow

Use AI to expand and test possibilities, then evaluate against real learning goals.
